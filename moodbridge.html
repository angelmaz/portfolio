MoodBridge 2.0: Ethical Intelligence-Augmentation System

An interaction architecture for emotional regulation designed to reduce decision fatigue, protect user autonomy, and replace prescriptive AI automation with contextual intelligence augmentation.

Course & Context Stanford Online — UI/UX Design for AI Products
Role Product Designer (AI Interaction, UX Research, Ethical Safeguards)
Paradigm Intelligence Augmentation (IA) • Human-in-the-Loop

1. The Problem: Decision Fatigue in Low-Energy States

People experiencing stress or low mood can face increased decision fatigue. When emotional regulation apps present extensive menus of 30-minute meditations, complex journaling prompts, or rigid streak goals, the cognitive effort required to choose can make engagement more difficult.

Furthermore, many digital wellness applications rely on overly cheerful or prescriptive wellness language, which users can perceive as patronizing or artificial during moments of distress.

Core Research Insight: Participants described decision-making as significantly harder during low-energy or stressed states. When overwhelmed, users prefer exactly one low-friction micro-action over expansive catalogs of self-care options.

2. Research-Informed Design Principles

Three exploratory interviews shaped the core interaction model. Participants consistently expressed a desire for minimal interaction overhead, grounded tone, greater control over personal data, and activities that could be completed in 1 to 5 minutes.

1. One Suggestion at a Time

Eliminates decision paralysis by offering a single, contextual micro-action instead of an extensive catalog.

2. 1–5 Minute Micro-Actions

Low-friction activities (e.g., box breathing, gentle posture reset) designed to be achievable during low-energy moments.

3. Grounded & Neutral Tone

Replaces forced cheerfulness with calm, objective language to maintain user trust and respect emotional reality.

4. User Agency: Accept / Modify / Skip

Ensures zero penalty or guilt mechanics when users choose not to engage or request an alternative difficulty.

3. Core Interaction Architecture

MoodBridge implements a Mixed-Initiative Interaction model. The user provides a lightweight check-in, the system proposes a single contextual micro-action, and the user retains full authority over how to proceed.

Core Wireframe Architecture: Minimal check-in prompt leading directly to a single micro-action with instant user controls (Accept, Modify, Skip).
  • Accept: Initiates a brief, distraction-free guided timer for the suggested action.
  • Modify: Instantly swaps the suggestion for a lower-intensity alternative without requiring conversational text prompts.
  • Skip: Closes the session cleanly with zero streak loss, negative progress indicators, or guilt triggers.

4. Prescriptive Automation vs. Augmented Intelligence

During the design exploration, I compared a rigid automation approach against an intelligence-augmentation model that preserves human agency:

Dimension Prescriptive Automation Intelligence Augmentation (MoodBridge)
Control System determines next step AI proposes → User decides
Cognitive Load Multiple prompts & routines One contextual suggestion
Flexibility Fixed recommendation flow User Agency (Accept / Modify / Skip)
Tone Directive or overly cheerful Neutral, calm, and non-judgmental

5. Responsible AI, Privacy & Escalation Safeguards

Designing for personal emotional regulation requires strict ethical boundaries and clear risk mitigation:

  • Local-First Privacy: Check-in data is designed to remain on-device by default, reducing unnecessary cloud exposure and giving users greater control over sensitive personal data.
  • Non-Diagnostic Scope: The system is strictly positioned as a personal wellbeing companion, avoiding clinical terminology, medical assertions, or simulated therapeutic advice.
  • Responsible Escalation: If a user repeatedly reports high distress across consecutive check-ins, the system can surface optional professional support resources and crisis helplines without diagnosing the user or forcing an escalation.

6. Project Constraints & What I'd Test Next

Project Context: MoodBridge was developed as a conceptual capstone project within the Stanford Online UI/UX Design for AI Products program. The interaction models, responsible-AI safeguards, and privacy approaches represent design directions rather than a deployed clinical software application.

If taken into active development, future validation steps would include:

  • Testing micro-action completion and perceived utility across varying fatigue levels in longitudinal diary studies.
  • Evaluating user comprehension of local vs. cloud data storage options during onboarding.
  • Refining alternative suggestion categories to ensure the "Modify" button delivers meaningful variety without reintroducing decision paralysis.
